Root cause of the "messy navigation". In Shopify the same job is done by tags + metafields + automated collections + native filters — one product, one source of truth, appearing wherever the rules say it belongs, with zero manual upkeep.

We design the store around a single customer journey and then assign every stage a concrete surface on the site. Nothing gets built that doesn't serve a stage.

Navigation & search — from selling categories to selling solutions

This is the single biggest change, and the reason a rebuild beats a re-skin. We move from a menu that lists catalogue names to a menu that advises. A first-time visitor who has never heard of a TriLight product name must still land on the right bottle in two clicks.

4.1 The new menu

Menu entryContents
Shop by NeedPrimary entry point. Grouped concerns: Immune & Cold/Flu · Cough & Respiratory · Allergies & Sinus · Digestion · Sleep, Stress & Anxiety · Focus & ADHD · Skin · Joint & Pain · Detox & Cleanse · Energy · Women's Health.
Shop by Who It's ForChildren · Pregnancy & New Baby · Adults · Whole Family. Kept as a filter dimension, not a duplicate tree.
Best Sellers & KitsBest sellers, wellness kits, weekly specials, volume discounts.
Find Your FormulaThe guided shopping entry (Section 6).
LearnThe 291 migrated articles, reorganised by concern so content feeds product pages.
About / Since 1991Brand story, the 3-stage liquid process, wholesale enquiry.

Menu items carry short advisory copy and imagery inside the mega menu — the menu itself does part of the consulting, which is what lifts conversion for first-time visitors.

4.2 How it's built (and why it stops getting messy)

  • One product record, many placements. Each product gets structured tags and metafields — concern, audience, age-range, format, size, dietary. Collections are automated on those rules, so ~20 curated collections replace 98 hand-maintained category pages. Adding a concern later is a tag, not a new page.
  • Native filters. Shopify Search & Discovery (free, first-party) delivers filtering by need, audience, age, format, size and price — the exact thing that required custom development on BigCommerce.
  • Search that understands intent. Synonym groups and search boosts so "good for hair", "won't sleep", "tummy ache", "ear infection" and misspelled formula names all resolve to real products. Product-name shoppers get the product plus need-based related items.
  • No orphan categories. The 98 old category URLs collapse into ~20 collections. Designing that structure also produces the legacy-category → collection redirect map, which LitExtension executes — no equity lost, no dead ends.

6

Filters, related products, upsell & guided shopping

6.1 Filters

Shopify Search & Discovery (free, first-party) — filters configured on the metafield/tag model from Section 4.2: concern, audience, age range, format, bottle size, dietary attributes and price. This is native, no custom filter code, and TriLight's team can edit filter sets themselves in the admin. This alone removes the largest maintenance burden they had on BigCommerce.

6.2 Related products & upsell

  • Related / complete-the-routine — theme-native related products, driven by shared concern and audience tags rather than generic "same collection" logic.
  • Frequently Bought Together — Frequently Bought Together (Code Black Belt) on the product page for one-click bundle add-to-cart with bundle discounts. Free tier allows 3 manual bundles; $14.99/mo (to 50 orders/mo), $19.99/mo (to 500 orders/mo) or $39.99/mo unlock unlimited and automatic AI recommendations.
  • Cart & post-purchase — theme cart-drawer recommendations and Shopify's native post-purchase page offers for low-friction add-ons.
  • Kits — the existing wellness kits become the natural upsell for anyone buying a single related formula.

6.3 Guided shopping

The flow, built from native collection and filter mechanics rather than a black-box app:

Shop by Need → pick the concern → pick who it's for (child / pregnancy / adult) → filtered result set of 2–5 suitable formulas with plain-language "what this is for" copy → product page with dosage-by-age and ingredients → related & frequently-bought-together → cart.

Two or three choices, always fewer than five results at the end. A customer who cannot name a single TriLight product still reaches the right bottle. Because it's built on collections and native filters, every step is also a linkable, indexable URL — the guided path doubles as SEO landing pages, which a JavaScript quiz app would not give us.

Judge.me + Smile + Omnisend as one loop, not three apps

The requested model — buy → review → get rewarded → buy again → refer a friend → new customer — is a closed loop. We wire it as one flywheel where each app triggers the next, rather than three separate installs.

StepTriggerSystemWhat the customer sees
1Order fulfilledShopify → OmnisendOrder & shipping confirmation, then a "how to take it" onboarding email keyed to the product bought.
2Delivery + N daysJudge.meReview request timed to bottle size, with photo/video upload and points clearly promised.
3Review submittedJudge.me → SmilePoints land automatically; confirmation states the balance and what it can be redeemed for.
4Points earnedSmile → Omnisend"You have X points" email with a one-click path back to a relevant product.
5Replenishment windowOmnisendReorder reminder timed to expected bottle duration, with points balance as the nudge.
6Post-review / post-reorderSmile referralGive a Friend a Free 2oz Bottle — personal referral link; the friend gets a free bottle, the referrer gets points.
7Friend redeemsSmile → OmnisendNew customer enters at step 1; referrer is thanked and re-engaged. Loop closes.

On-site, the same loop is visible rather than buried: points balance in the header for logged-in members, points-to-earn on the product page, review prompts on the order-status page, and the referral offer in the footer and post-purchase page.

Two things worth knowing before you pick plans.
  • The free-bottle referral needs a paid Smile tier. Smile's Free plan covers points, rewards and referrals up to 200 monthly orders, but free product rewards start on Essential ($15/mo, up to 500 orders/mo). So "Give a Friend a Free 2oz Bottle" as a literal free product means Essential at minimum. Essential also allows one integration (which we'd point at Omnisend). Standard ($79/mo) adds product-page points display and thank-you-page promotions — nice, not required at launch.
  • Smile is already running on BigCommerce. Existing member points balances and history need an explicit migration step with Smile before launch, so no customer loses accrued points on switch-over day. We'll raise this with Smile support as part of the build; please confirm the current plan and member count.

Google Shopping, Merchant Center, product feed & multichannel

Shopify handles multichannel through native sales channels, so the work is not "building feeds" — it's getting the product data model right once so every channel accepts it. This is the part that must be done as the catalogue lands, not after.

9.1 Data model first

  • Variants — bottle sizes (2oz / 4oz / 8oz) as proper variants with distinct SKUs and barcodes, not as separate products. This is what lets Google, Meta and the Shop app group and price them correctly.
  • Required feed fields — GTIN/UPC or MPN, brand, condition, product category (Shopify's standard taxonomy), Google product category, age group, and clean per-variant images.
  • Metafields for meaning — concern, audience, dosage, ingredients, dietary flags. These drive the storefront and supply the structured attributes newer surfaces read.
  • Structured data — Product, Offer, AggregateRating (from Judge.me), Article and Breadcrumb schema on every relevant template. This is what makes the catalogue legible to Google, to the Shop app, and to the emerging AI/agentic shopping surfaces Shopify is exposing catalogues to.

Compliance flag, raised early on purpose. Herbal and health-supplement listings are a restricted category on Google Shopping and Meta. Product titles, descriptions and any implied health claims must be worded to their policies or the feed gets disapproved — this is a content constraint, not a technical one.
