You're moving Outdoor Zone from WooCommerce to Shopify, keeping your logo, colours and overall tone, and you asked for two options: replicate the current site, or redesign it. We went through the live site page by page. The catalogue is large (about 1,000 products in 79 categories), so the move needs care, but it's routine. The bigger question is how the store looks. Today it reads as a general shop rather than a specialist airsoft and tactical store. Below are both options, what we found on the current site, and the few things we need from you before the final number is locked (§4).

1

Two ways to make the move

1.1 Both options include

  • Setting up the Shopify store on the Ella theme (§5) with your existing logo, colours and visual tone
  • Your current menu rebuilt as Shopify collections and a mega menu, reshaped from four levels to three (§3.2)
  • Moving the catalogue: about 1,000 products with their variants, prices, images and brands, 79 categories, and the content pages, plus redirects from every old URL so search rankings and shared links keep working (§3.1)
  • Fixing the issues in §2 marked "Both"
  • Full QA on desktop and mobile (cart, checkout and every page type) before launch

1.2 Option 1 — Replicate ($1,350, 45h)

We rebuild the current pages and sections on Shopify in the same order, with the same brand. Along the way we fix the things that make the store look unfinished: spacing between homepage sections, the slider dots, the out-of-stock buttons, the collection filters, the product gallery, the mobile header and menu, and the partner logos. The homepage keeps its current structure and look.

1.3 Option 2 — Redesign ($1,950, 65h) — recommended

Everything in Option 1, plus a dedicated redesign phase (+20h). Same logo and colours, but a look that fits what you sell: a full-width hero with a strong headline and a clear call to action, category tiles with photography, a product card designed for gear (brand, price, stock status and badges in a fixed layout), bolder type, and a tidier announcement bar. One round of revisions is included.

We recommend this option. Airsoft and tactical buyers expect a store that looks rugged and specialist, like the leading stores in your market. The current site uses soft, general-purpose styling that undersells the range. Since you're changing platform anyway, this is the cheapest moment to fix it: the difference is $600.

3

What changes moving off WooCommerce

This applies to both options.

3.1 Catalogue and content

From the site's sitemap: about 1,000 products, 79 categories and 22 brand pages, plus the content pages (About us, Contact, Shipping and returns, Terms, Privacy, Cookie policy, and "Airsoft — A Complete Guide"). There is no blog to move.

At this size the data is moved with LitExtension, a migration service you buy directly. Its price depends on the number of products, customers and orders you choose to move. We set up the mapping (categories to collections, brands to vendors and filters, sizes and variants), run the checks and fix what doesn't land correctly. Customers and order history can move in the same run if you want them (§4).

3.2 Menu: four levels become three

Your main menu goes four levels deep: Shop → Airsoft Accessories and Parts → Batteries and chargers → Batteries. Shopify menus support three. The simplest fix loses nothing: we drop the "Shop" level and put your nine main categories (Airsoft Guns, Accessories and Parts, BBs Gas & Lubricants, HPA, Tactical Gear, Outdoor Clothing, Knives and Outdoor Gear, New Arrivals, Sale) straight into the top menu. Every category and sub-category stays where customers expect it. We'll confirm the final structure with you before building.

3.3 Product filter and search

Your current filter plugin and live search come across as Shopify's built-in search and filters, which cover brand, price, availability and product type without an extra app.

3.4 WhatsApp chat

The "I've got your six" WhatsApp button carries over with a free Shopify app, keeping the same wording.

Who configures what

Check your payment provider first. Shopify Payments doesn't accept weapons, and airsoft replicas are usually treated as weapons, so expect to use a third-party payment provider that accepts airsoft. When you do, Shopify adds its own transaction fee on top of the provider's fee (2% on Basic, 1% on Grow, 0.6% on Advanced). We'll help you compare providers and plans before launch. It's a business decision, not a build one.

Payment and shipping are configured by you, in your own Shopify admin. We advise, document and verify, but we don't enter the credentials.

We do this on purpose. Connecting a payment provider means handling merchant credentials and API keys that give financial access to your business, and a shipping account carries your commercial rate agreement. Those belong with the account holder, not an agency. Everything else in this proposal is built by us as normal.
